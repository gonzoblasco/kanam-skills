# Worldbuilding - Narrative Content Reference

Guide to building believable fictional worlds.

## Worldbuilding Bible Structure

```markdown
# [World Name] Bible

## Core Concept
- Genre:
- Tone:
- Central theme:
- What makes this world unique:

## Geography
- Map (reference or description)
- Climate zones
- Key locations
- Natural resources
- Borders and barriers

## History
- Origin myth / creation story
- Major eras
- Key events timeline
- Wars, disasters, discoveries

## Society
- Social structure / hierarchy
- Government type
- Laws and justice
- Economy and trade
- Education
- Technology level

## Culture
- Language(s)
- Religion / belief systems
- Art, music, literature
- Customs and traditions
- Food and drink
- Clothing and fashion

## Magic / Technology
- Rules and limitations
- Who can use it
- Cost of using it
- How it's perceived
- How it affects daily life

## Factions
- [Faction 1]: goals, members, territory, conflict
- [Faction 2]: goals, members, territory, conflict
```

## Consistency Checklist

- [ ] Magic/system has clear rules and costs
- [ ] Technology level is consistent across regions
- [ ] Economy makes sense (who grows food? who trades?)
- [ ] History explains current conflicts
- [ ] Culture affects character behavior
- [ ] Geography affects travel and communication
- [ ] Power structures have logical basis
- [ ] World feels lived-in, not designed

## Show, Don't Tell

| Tell | Show |
|---|---|
| "The kingdom is poor" | Characters barter, roads are unpaved, children are barefoot |
| "Magic is forbidden" | A character hides their abilities, uses euphemisms |
| "The city is ancient" | Architecture has layers, different eras visible |
| "Society is hierarchical" | Characters use formal address, defer to higher ranks |
